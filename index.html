The Science

Minerals that reach the cell.

Minerals are not just bones and shells. Inside each cell they carry electrical signals, power enzymes, build DNA and defend against oxidative damage. None of that happens unless the mineral first survives the gut and gets across the gut wall.

In a chelate, a trace metal is bound to amino acids, which helps protect it from binding with other feed components in the gut so it can be taken up where the animal needs it.

See the evidence

In the feed

A mineral is only as useful as the form it arrives in. Phytate, fibre and other minerals can tie it up.

In the gut

A chelated metal stays protected until it reaches the gut wall, where it can be absorbed.

At the cell

Taken up, the mineral becomes part of an enzyme, a hormone, a signal or a structure.

Mineral functions

Twelve minerals, one cell.

Macro minerals are needed in larger amounts and shape the cell’s structure, charge and energy. Trace minerals are needed in tiny amounts, usually as the working atom of an enzyme or hormone.

Ca

Calcium

Macro mineral

At the cellCa²⁺ is a second messenger: a rise inside the cell triggers muscle contraction, nerve signalling and enzyme activation.

  • Bone, teeth, shell and eggshell formation
  • Blood clotting
  • Milk and egg production

Works as a pair with phosphorus; the ratio matters as much as the amount.

P

Phosphorus

Macro mineral

At the cellForms the backbone of ATP, DNA, RNA and the phospholipids of every cell membrane.

  • Bone mineral together with calcium
  • Energy transfer and storage (ATP)
  • Acid–base buffering

Plant feeds hold much of it as phytate, which many animals digest poorly.

Na

Sodium

Macro mineral

At the cellThe Na⁺/K⁺ pump builds the gradient that drives nerve impulses and nutrient uptake into cells.

  • Fluid and osmotic balance
  • Sodium-coupled uptake of glucose and amino acids
  • Nerve and muscle excitability

Especially important for fish and shrimp regulating salts in water of differing salinity.

Mg

Magnesium

Macro mineral

At the cellEvery ATP-using reaction needs Mg²⁺ bound to ATP; it also activates hundreds of enzymes.

  • Muscle relaxation and nerve function
  • Protein and nucleic-acid synthesis
  • Bone structure

Low intake on lush grass can cause grass tetany in ruminants.

K

Potassium

Macro mineral

At the cellThe main positive ion inside cells; with sodium it sets the resting membrane potential.

  • Nerve and muscle function
  • Fluid and acid–base balance
  • Enzyme activation

Often a focus during heat stress, when electrolytes are lost.

Zn

Zinc

Trace mineral

At the cellBuilt into hundreds of enzymes and into zinc-finger proteins that read DNA.

  • Immune function
  • Growth and tissue repair
  • Skin, coat and feather quality
  • Reproduction

A staple of piglet, broiler and shrimp diets.

Cu

Copper

Trace mineral

At the cellPart of cytochrome c oxidase in mitochondria and of Cu/Zn superoxide dismutase.

  • Iron use and red-cell formation
  • Collagen and elastin cross-linking
  • Pigmentation
  • Oxygen carrier haemocyanin in shrimp and other crustaceans

Too little or too much both cause harm; the window is narrow in some species such as sheep.

Mn

Manganese

Trace mineral

At the cellPowers mitochondrial superoxide dismutase (MnSOD), which protects the cell’s energy plant.

  • Cartilage, bone and shell matrix
  • Eggshell strength
  • Carbohydrate and fat metabolism

A key mineral for poultry legs and eggshells.

Fe

Iron

Trace mineral

At the cellSits in the haem and iron–sulfur groups of the respiratory chain and many enzymes.

  • Haemoglobin and myoglobin: oxygen transport and storage
  • Energy metabolism
  • Immune defence

Newborn piglets are born with low stores and are commonly supplemented.

Co

Cobalt

Trace mineral

At the cellThe core atom of vitamin B12 (cobalamin), needed by two key enzymes.

  • Red blood cell formation
  • Normal nerve and energy metabolism

Ruminant gut microbes need cobalt to make their own B12.

I

Iodine

Trace mineral

At the cellAn essential part of thyroid hormones T3 and T4, which tune gene activity and metabolic rate.

  • Metabolic rate
  • Growth and development
  • Reproduction

Needs differ widely by diet and region; goitrogens in some feeds raise requirements.

Se

Selenium

Trace mineral

At the cellSits in the amino acid selenocysteine, found in glutathione peroxidases that clear peroxides.

  • Antioxidant defence alongside vitamin E
  • Activation of thyroid hormone
  • Immune response and fertility
  • Muscle health

A classic partner mineral to vitamin E; the gap between enough and too much is small.

General nutrition information. Requirements and safe upper limits differ by species, life stage and diet.

FAQ

What is a chelated mineral?

A trace mineral bound to amino acids, so it stays protected in the gut and is easier for the animal to absorb.

What is the difference between macro and trace minerals?

Macro minerals (Ca, P, Na, Mg, K) are needed in larger amounts, usually grams per kilogram of feed. Trace minerals (Zn, Cu, Mn, Fe, Co, I, Se) are needed in milligrams or less, but each one has a specific job that nothing else can do.

Which species do you cover?

Aquaculture, poultry, swine, ruminants and companion animals. Tell us your species and we will advise.

Can you help with minerals beyond zinc, copper, manganese, iron and cobalt?

Yes, get in touch with the minerals you are balancing, such as selenium, iodine or magnesium, and we will advise on what fits your formulation.
